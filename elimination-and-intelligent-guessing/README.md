# Elimination Problems

## Difficulty Guide

- `(*)` Foundational level (quick single-step reasoning)
- `(**)` Intermediate level (2-step or mixed-concept reasoning)
- `(***)` Challenge level (multi-step selective-school style problems)

## Question 1 (*)

Each school sent 3 runners in a team cross country race. Alice, Belinda and Clara were the three representatives for their school. Alice finished the race in middle position, Belinda finished after Alice, in 19th position, and Clara finished 28th. How many runners took part in the race?

### Answer

- [ ] 27
- [ ] 30
- [x] 33
- [ ] 36

### Explanation

Alice finishing in middle position implies that there were an odd number of participants, which eliminates 30 and 36. Given that Clara finished 28th, there must be more than 28 participants, so 33 is the answer.

## Question 2 (**)

A farmer has some chickens and some goats. Together there are 116 heads and 282 legs. How many chickens and goats does the farmer have?

### Answer

- [ ] 18 goats and 125 chickens
- [ ] 25 goats and 100 chickens
- [ ] 20 goats and 110 chickens
- [x] 25 goats and 91 chickens

### Explanation

This problem is also part of the [chicken-and-goat](../chicken-and-goat/README.md) problems. It can be solved quickly by elimination. The number of heads is 116, so the sum of chickens and goats in the answers must be 116. Only the last option satisfies this condition.

## Question 3 (*)

What is the value of 10% + √(49%)?

### Answer

- [ ] 60%
- [ ] 70%
- [x] 80%
- [ ] 90%

### Explanation

10% = 0.10.

√(49%) = √(0.49) = 0.7 = 70%.

Therefore, 10% + √(49%) = 10% + 70% = 80%.

## Question 4 (*)

The average of 5 consecutive integers is 32. What is the largest integer?

### Answer

- [ ] 33
- [ ] 35
- [x] 34
- [ ] 36

### Explanation

For 5 consecutive integers, the average is the middle number.

So the numbers are 30, 31, 32, 33, 34.

Largest integer is 34.

## Question 5 (**)

A number leaves remainder 2 when divided by 5 and remainder 3 when divided by 4. Which of the following could be the number?

### Answer

- [x] 27
- [ ] 18
- [ ] 22
- [ ] 32

### Explanation

Check options quickly:

27 ÷ 5 leaves remainder 2, and 27 ÷ 4 leaves remainder 3.

So 27 satisfies both conditions.

## Question 6 (**)

Without fully multiplying, what is the units digit of $37 \times 48 \times 29$?

### Answer

- [ ] 2
- [ ] 6
- [ ] 8
- [x] 4

### Explanation

Only units digits matter: $7 \times 8 \times 9$.

$7 \times 8 = 56$ (units digit 6).

$6 \times 9 = 54$ (units digit 4).

So units digit is 4.

## Question 7 (**)

One of these fractions is greater than $\frac{3}{4}$. Which one?

### Answer

- [ ] $\frac{6}{9}$
- [ ] $\frac{9}{12}$
- [x] $\frac{13}{16}$
- [ ] $\frac{15}{20}$

### Explanation

$\frac{3}{4} = 0.75$.

$\frac{6}{9} = \frac{2}{3} < 0.75$.

$\frac{9}{12} = \frac{3}{4}$ (equal, not greater).

$\frac{13}{16} = 0.8125 > 0.75$.

$\frac{15}{20} = \frac{3}{4}$ (equal).

## Question 8 (**)

The sum of two numbers is 45. One number is 9 more than the other. What is the larger number?

### Answer

- [ ] 24
- [ ] 25
- [x] 27
- [ ] 28

### Explanation

If two numbers differ by 9 and sum to 45, split 45 equally first: $45/2 = 22.5$.

Then move half the difference ($9/2 = 4.5$):

Larger number = $22.5 + 4.5 = 27$.
